Hi, I'm

John Johnsson

Systems administrator · reader · tinkerer

Gothenburg, Sweden

About me

I'm a systems administrator with over a decade of experience in infrastructure and platform engineering. Most of my career has been spent keeping servers healthy, automating the tedious parts, and being the person who picks up the 2 am alert that turns out to be a flaky cronjob. I've lived in Gothenburg my whole life — born here in 1981 and never found a good enough reason to leave.

I first got into Linux when I was sixteen, installing Slackware on a second-hand Compaq tower that my uncle gave me in 1997. Something about the terminal just clicked. These days my work revolves around bastion hosts, backup pipelines, and making sure core routing stays up. Outside of work I read a lot of science fiction, restore old mechanical keyboards, and try not to ruin whatever I'm currently brewing.

What I'm reading

Science fiction has been my thing since I was a teenager. My gateway was Isaac Asimov's Foundation — I borrowed it from the school library in 1996 and never really looked back. That led me to Arthur C. Clarke's Rendezvous with Rama, then to Ursula K. Le Guin's The Dispossessed (1974), and eventually to the author who ended up shaping my taste more than any other: Frank Herbert.

I own every novel Herbert ever published, most of them in battered paperback. People always assume my favorite is Dune, and sure, it's a masterpiece, but the one I keep coming back to is God Emperor of Dune — the fourth book in the series. It was published in 1981, the same year I was born, and I've always felt a quiet, probably irrational attachment to that coincidence. There's a philosophical weight to that book — Leto's transformation, the Golden Path, the sheer scope of it — that the earlier novels don't quite reach. Herbert was operating on a completely different level by that point.

I also went through a serious William Gibson phase in university. Neuromancer (1984) was a revelation at the time — I remember reading the opening line about the sky being the color of television and thinking this is what science fiction should feel like. After Gibson I got into Neal Stephenson; Snow Crash (1992) was wild, and Cryptonomicon has been sitting on my nightstand for two months now, but I keep getting pulled back into Herbert re-reads. A colleague once told me I should just build a dictionary of every term Herbert ever invented and be done with it. She might have a point — the man built entire lexicons for his worlds.

My bookshelves at home are complete chaos. Science fiction novels sit next to O'Reilly technical books, a Swedish-English dictionary from the 1920s that belonged to my grandfather, a biography of Margaret Hamilton I keep meaning to finish, and whatever my partner has lent me that month.

Tinkering

Keyboards

There's always a half-disassembled keyboard on my desk. I've been collecting and restoring vintage mechanical boards for about five years now — mostly Cherry and Alps switch models from the eighties. My daily driver is a 1987 IBM Model M that I picked up at a flea market in Malmö for almost nothing. I've replaced the springs and given it a full cleaning, and it types like the day it left the factory. I'm also tempted by Topre boards, but I keep coming back to buckling springs. There's a reason that design survived forty years.

Homebrewing

I started brewing in 2019 after a friend dragged me to a craft beer festival in Copenhagen. My first attempt — an overly ambitious Pilsner — was memorably bad. My partner diplomatically described it as "interesting." I've improved since then. Current project: a Baltic Porter that I'm aiming to have ready by December. I've also been experimenting with a Weissbier recipe that I got from a colleague in Munich. The specific gravity readings are promising, but I've learned not to get excited until the final pour.

Homelab

My home setup is a small Proxmox cluster running on three refurbished Dell Optiplex machines. Nothing fancy — just enough to run a Gitea instance, a Grafana dashboard for monitoring my network, and a Minecraft server that my nephew uses approximately once a month. I keep telling myself I'll set up a proper Ansible playbook for provisioning, but honestly the manual approach works fine for three nodes.
